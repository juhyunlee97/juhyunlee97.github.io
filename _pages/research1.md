Neuroscience is essential for advancing our understanding of the brain and nervous system. Yet, conventional experimental approaches often depend on tethered systems with bulky equipment, limiting studies of naturalistic behavior in animal models. <br>
To address this limitation, my research focuses on wireless neural interfaces, particularly leveraging optogenetics. I have proposed a rapid and low-cost fabrication method for optogenetic probes ([Nature Protocols, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41596-022-00758-8); [Advanced Functional Materials, 2020](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/adfm.202004285)) and have contributed to an [open-source platform](https://www.wirelessprobe.org/) enabling broad adoption of wireless optogenetics. <br>
Currently, my work extends beyond neural stimulation to the development of wireless systems capable of monitoring diverse forms of neural activity in real time.
