Neuroscience plays a critical role in advancing our understanding of the brain and nervous system. <br>
However, most conventional experimental approaches rely on tethered systems with bulky equipment, which significantly limit the ability to study naturalistic behavior in animal models. <br>
To address this limitation, my research focuses on wireless neural interfaces, particularly leveraging optogenetics—a powerful technique that enables cell-type-specific and temporally precise neuromodulation. <br>
I have proposed a rapid and low-cost fabrication method for optogenetic probes ([Nature Protocols, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41596-022-00758-8); [Advanced Functional Materials, 2020](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/adfm.202004285)) and have contributed to the establishment of an [open-source platform](https://www.wirelessprobe.org/) that allows neuroscience researchers to easily adopt wireless optogenetic technologies. <br>
Currently, my work extends beyond neural stimulation to the development of soft, wireless systems capable of monitoring diverse forms of neural activity in real time.
